Market Insights & Guides

Last updated: 24 August 2026

Understanding what you see on the NEXUS Markets dashboard makes it far more useful. These short, plain-English guides explain how the world's markets fit together — the trading sessions, what moves gold, how crypto trades around the clock, and how to read the economic calendar.

Understanding the global trading sessions

The foreign-exchange market runs 24 hours a day, five days a week, because it is really a chain of regional markets that hand over to one another as the day moves around the planet. Trading opens in Sydney, flows into Tokyo and the wider Asian session, passes to London — the largest FX centre in the world — and finally to New York before the cycle begins again.

The busiest and most liquid hours are usually the London–New York overlap, when two major financial centres are open at the same time. Liquidity tends to be thinner during the late Asian session, which can mean wider spreads. The session panel on NEXUS Markets shows each market's local open and close times converted to your own timezone, with a live countdown, so you can see at a glance which centres are active right now.

What moves the price of gold

Gold is priced in US dollars per troy ounce, so the first thing that moves it is the strength of the dollar — a weaker dollar usually lifts gold, and a stronger dollar tends to weigh on it. Gold is also a classic safe-haven asset: during periods of geopolitical tension, market stress or high inflation, investors often move money into gold, pushing prices up.

Interest rates matter too. Because gold pays no yield, higher interest rates make income-producing assets relatively more attractive and can pressure gold, while rate cuts often support it. Silver, platinum and palladium — also shown on the dashboard — share some of these drivers but have a larger industrial-demand component, which makes them more sensitive to the health of the global economy.

Cryptocurrency: a market that never sleeps

Unlike forex and metals, cryptocurrency trades 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including weekends and holidays. Bitcoin and Ethereum are the two largest assets by market value and often set the tone for the rest of the market; when Bitcoin moves sharply, smaller "altcoins" frequently follow.

Crypto is known for its volatility — double-digit percentage moves in a single day are not unusual. Prices are driven by a mix of supply-and-demand, regulation news, large institutional flows, network upgrades and overall market sentiment. The NEXUS Markets crypto panel streams live prices tick-by-tick, and the 24-hour change figure tells you how far an asset has moved over the last day.

How to read the economic calendar

The economic calendar lists scheduled data releases — things like inflation figures, employment reports and central-bank interest-rate decisions — that can move currencies, metals and even crypto. Each event usually shows three numbers: the Previous reading, the market's Forecast, and, once released, the Actual result.

The reaction in the market is often driven by the surprise — the gap between the actual number and the forecast. A result that beats expectations can strengthen a currency, while a miss can weaken it. High-impact events (marked in red) tend to cause the sharpest short-term volatility, so many traders are especially careful around them. NEXUS Markets shows a live countdown in the final minutes before each release and the actual result immediately afterwards.

The major forex pairs

A handful of "major" currency pairs account for most of global FX trading. EUR/USD (euro vs US dollar) is the most traded pair in the world; GBP/USD and USD/JPY are also heavily traded. Pairs that do not include the US dollar, such as EUR/GBP, are called "crosses." The number you see quoted is how much of the second currency one unit of the first is worth — so EUR/USD at 1.10 means one euro buys 1.10 US dollars.
